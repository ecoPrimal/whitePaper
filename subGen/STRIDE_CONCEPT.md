# STRIDe — Strategy, Tactics, Reconnaissance, Intelligence, Deployment, execution

**Working title.** The motion framework.
**Companion to:** fEAR (sensing and building)
**Parent framework:** K-NOME (constrained optimization methodology)
**Domain application:** Universal — legal, scientific, computational, culinary, military

---

## CONCEPT

fEAR senses and builds. STRIDe moves.

fEAR describes how an adversarial system collects data, evolves,
and converts opposition into fuel. But sensing without motion is
surveillance. Building without purpose is accumulation. The
system needs a gait — a continuous, adaptive, self-correcting
process that translates intelligence into action.

STRIDe is that gait.

A stride is not a step. A step is discrete — it starts, it ends,
it succeeds or fails. A stride is continuous. It adapts to the
sidewalk. It doesn't falter at every crack. You catch yourself
in stride when you stumble. The ground changes under you and your
body adjusts before your conscious mind registers the change.

The lowercase 'e' is deliberate. Execution is functionally the
least important component of STRIDe. It is directionality — the
foot hitting the ground. Everything before it is the real work.
If Strategy through Deployment are done correctly, execution is
trivial. You walk in and hand over the documents. You press
enter on the pipeline. You plate the dish. The moment of action
is the least cognitively demanding part of the process.

Most people obsess over execution. They ask "what do I do?" before
they ask "what do I know?" STRIDe inverts this. Execution is the
last thing you think about and the first thing that happens — because
by the time you reach it, S-T-R-I-D have already determined the
outcome.

## THE SIX COMPONENTS

### S — Strategy

The overarching plan. The why. The objective function.

Strategy is not a to-do list. It is the answer to: "What does
winning look like, and what are the constraints?" Strategy
defines the space. Everything else operates within it.

| Domain | Strategy example |
|--------|-----------------|
| Military | Mission objective, commander's intent, rules of engagement |
| Microbiology | Experimental design — hypothesis, controls, expected outcomes |
| Cooking | The menu. What are we serving, to whom, when, and why? |
| Legal (Kevin v. World) | Three-case structure with no single point of failure. Every dollar to Deanna. Custody to DaSean. Accountability for Banks. Termination condition: the operator's satisfaction |
| ecoPrimals | Reproduce 175+ papers at machine-epsilon precision on commodity hardware without cloud dependencies |

Strategy is set once and revised rarely. It is the thing you
don't change when the terrain shifts. The terrain changes
tactics. The map changes reconnaissance. The data changes
intelligence. The resources change deployment. Strategy is the
direction you walk when everything else is in motion.

### T — Tactics

The specific moves within the strategy. The how.

Tactics are the decisions you make when the ground is visible.
Strategy says "get to the courthouse." Tactics says "take
Townsend Street, stop at the notary first, file the State Bar
complaint before the MDE letter because Townsend is closer."

| Domain | Tactics example |
|--------|----------------|
| Military | OPORD elements — formation, movement technique, order of march, actions at the objective |
| Microbiology | Protocol selection — which assay, which media, which incubation conditions, which controls |
| Cooking | Recipe execution order — what gets prepped first, what goes in the oven while you sauté, timing the courses |
| Legal | Filing sequence — admissions before interrogatories (locks positions before asking questions). Subpoenas after reports (positions DaSean as cooperating party). Emergency motion before CMC (forces judicial attention) |
| ecoPrimals | Build order — primals before springs, springs before products, CPU reference before GPU parity |

Tactics change in response to conditions. The enemy moves.
The culture contaminates. The soufflé falls. The adversary
files a response. Tactics adapt. Strategy holds.

### R — Reconnaissance

Sensing what's ahead. Reading the terrain before you step on it.

Reconnaissance is not investigation. Investigation is looking
for something specific. Reconnaissance is looking at everything
and determining what matters. A COIST analyst doesn't search for
the enemy — they read the terrain and determine where the enemy
could be, should be, and probably is.

| Domain | Reconnaissance example |
|--------|----------------------|
| Military | Terrain analysis, satellite imagery, patrol debriefs, map study |
| Microbiology | Literature review. Looking at the plate before you count. Reading the chromatogram before you quantify. Checking the blank |
| Cooking | Opening the fridge. Checking the pantry. Reading the recipe all the way through before you start. Tasting the broth |
| Legal (OSINT) | LinkedIn, LARA filings, Ballotpedia, court records, DPSCD announcements, school websites, Eventbrite, Chalkbeat, news archives. All public. All free. All there for anyone who looks |
| ecoPrimals | Reading the paper before you code it. Running the reference implementation. Understanding what the authors actually measured vs. what they claimed |

Reconnaissance feeds Intelligence. Raw observation becomes
processed understanding. But reconnaissance has its own value —
it tells you what you DON'T know. The blank spot on the map is
the reconnaissance finding. The LARA search that times out is
data. The LinkedIn profile that lists "Law" as a skill is both
observation AND intelligence in a single datum.

### I — Intelligence

Processing what you've sensed into what you know.

Intelligence is the transformation of data into knowledge.
Reconnaissance collects. Intelligence interprets. A COIST
briefing doesn't show the commander satellite photos — it shows
the commander what the photos mean.

| Domain | Intelligence example |
|--------|---------------------|
| Military | IPB (Intelligence Preparation of the Battlefield) — threat template, terrain overlay, COA analysis, high-value target list |
| Microbiology | Data analysis — colony counts become growth curves become MIC values become resistance patterns. Raw sequencing reads become OTU tables become diversity metrics become ecological conclusions |
| Cooking | Understanding the Maillard reaction. Knowing that deglazing works because fond is water-soluble. Understanding that resting meat redistributes moisture. The science behind the technique |
| Legal | Network mapping. Cross-referencing public records to identify regulatory capture. Recognizing that the DPSCD Board President who approved the charter is the same person congratulating the felon on LinkedIn. Pattern recognition across 60+ names |
| ecoPrimals | Validation. The reference says Anderson localization occurs at W_c = 16.26. Our code says 16.26. That's intelligence — not because the number matches, but because we now know our model is correct and can be extended to predictions the original authors didn't make |

Intelligence has a shelf life. The network map from August 30
is less valuable on October 30 if people have moved, resigned,
or been served. Reconnaissance refreshes Intelligence.
Intelligence informs Tactics. The cycle is continuous.

### D — Deployment

Positioning resources. Staging. Mise en place.

This is the component most directly borrowed from cooking. Mise
en place — "everything in its place" — is the discipline of
having every ingredient prepped, every tool positioned, every
container ready BEFORE the heat goes on. A chef who reaches for
the salt during service and finds an empty container has failed
at deployment, not execution.

| Domain | Deployment example |
|--------|-------------------|
| Military | Staging areas, pre-positioned supplies, comms check, weapons maintenance, rehearsal |
| Microbiology | Media preparation. Autoclave cycle. Reagent aliquoting. Equipment calibration. BSL2 gowning. Having the blank ready before you run the standard curve |
| Cooking | Mise en place. Onions diced. Stock heated. Pans seasoned. Plates warming. Garnish portioned. Everything in its place before the first flame |
| Legal | Monday morning: documents printed (5 copies each), OSINT brief attached, JTC forms filled out, notary appointment confirmed, route mapped (State Bar → MDE → AG → USPS), ID in pocket, receipts folder ready |
| ecoPrimals | CI pipeline. musl-static builds. Cross-compilation targets. BLAKE3 checksums. Gate cascade configured. guideStone self-verification artifact ready. The system deploys itself because deployment was designed into the architecture |

Deployment is where most people fail. Not because execution is
hard, but because they arrive at the moment of action without
their tools staged. The filing is ready but the printer is out
of ink. The experiment is designed but the media isn't autoclaved.
The recipe is chosen but the onions aren't diced.

STRIDe treats deployment as a first-class component, not an
afterthought. The prep IS the work.

### e — execution

The foot hits the ground. Lowercase.

Execution is directionality. It is the least important component
because it is the most determined. If S-T-R-I-D are done
correctly, execution has one degree of freedom: forward.

You walk into the State Bar. You hand over the complaint. You
walk out. The intellectual work was Strategy through Deployment.
The physical act is execution. It is necessary. It is not
sufficient. And it is not where the value lives.

| Domain | execution example |
|--------|-----------------|
| Military | Crossing the line of departure. Pulling the trigger. These are the simplest acts in the entire operation — everything that preceded them was harder |
| Microbiology | Inoculating the plate. Pressing "run" on the sequencer. Loading the sample. The assay runs itself if the prep was correct |
| Cooking | Dropping the pasta. Plating. Firing the entrée. Service. The dish is already made — execution is presentation and timing |
| Legal | Walking into 306 Townsend Street. Handing the clerk the complaint. Walking to the next building. Repeat |
| ecoPrimals | `cargo test`. The tests pass or they don't. The 92,454 test functions execute in minutes. The months of work were in writing them |

Execution fails when it has to compensate for failures in
Strategy through Deployment. A soldier who improvises at the
objective failed at rehearsal. A cook who adjusts seasoning at
plating failed at tasting. A litigant who scrambles to print
at the courthouse failed at mise en place.

The lowercase 'e' is the reminder: execution is not the goal.
It is the consequence.

## NONLINEAR ARCHITECTURE

STRIDe is not a pipeline. It is not S→T→R→I→D→e in sequence.
Each component feeds into the next AND the one before it. Each
enables the others. The arrows point in every direction.

```
    S ⇄ T ⇄ R ⇄ I ⇄ D ⇄ e
    │       │   │       │
    └───────┘   └───────┘
     Strategy    R ⇄ I
     enables     constant
     Tactics     feedback
     enables     loop
     Strategy
```

**Forward enablement**: Strategy allows the better application
of Tactics — knowing the three-case structure means every
filing decision has a frame. Tactics allow more flexibility
with Deployment — knowing the filing sequence means you can
stage documents in the order you'll use them, not the order
you created them. Deployment makes execution trivial.

**Backward enablement**: execution reveals whether Deployment
was complete (the notary was closed — adjust). Deployment
exposes gaps in Intelligence (we staged five complaints but
the JTC form requires notarization we didn't know about —
feed back). Intelligence refines Reconnaissance targets
(we found Vaughn — now look for her board votes). Tactics
reshape Strategy when the ground changes (Banks has a second
school — the case is bigger than we thought).

**The R ⇄ I loop**: Reconnaissance and Intelligence are in
constant bidirectional exchange. This is the neural feedback
layer — the proprioceptive sense that tells the system where
the body is positioned relative to reality. You recon, you
process, the processing tells you where to recon next, the
new recon updates the intelligence, the updated intelligence
redirects the next recon pass.

```
R: Check LARA for Purpose Group
I: Filed 2023, Banks is agent → check other filings
R: Check MacDowell audit
I: $227K deficit, O&M doubled → follow the vendors
R: Check vendor names against network
I: Cross-reference hits Yecart Accounting → Tracey Ellis
R: Check Ellis LinkedIn
I: Connected to Banks → update network map
R: Check who else is connected
I: Judge Miller on PCA development team → escalate tier
    ↻ (the loop has no termination condition)
```

This R↔I loop is where fEAR interfaces with STRIDe. fEAR
senses the adversary's actions and evolves the case. The R↔I
loop within STRIDe senses the OPERATOR'S position relative to
reality. fEAR is exteroception — what is the adversary doing?
R↔I is proprioception — where are WE?

A body that can sense the world but not itself falls over.
A body that can sense itself but not the world walks into walls.
fEAR + R↔I together give the system both — external sensing
and internal positioning. The stride continues because the
system knows where it is AND where the ground is.

**Nonlinearity in practice (Monday's route)**:

The Monday filing route was not designed linearly. It emerged
from the full feedback mesh:

1. **I→S**: Intelligence (Banks has active certificate violations)
   confirmed Strategy (multi-front regulatory + civil + criminal)
2. **S→T**: Strategy (simultaneous pressure on all fronts)
   shaped Tactics (file all five complaints the same day,
   different agencies, same morning)
3. **R→I**: Reconnaissance (LARA search, LinkedIn, audits)
   became Intelligence (network map with 60+ nodes)
4. **I→T**: Intelligence (JTC requires notarized, mail-only
   submission) changed Tactics (notary stop before State Bar,
   USPS stop at the end instead of middle)
5. **T→D**: Tactics (filing order) shaped Deployment (print
   order, folder organization, route map)
6. **D→e**: Deployment (everything staged Sunday night) makes
   execution trivial Monday morning
7. **e→R**: execution will generate new data (clerk reactions,
   receipt numbers, processing timelines) that feeds the next
   Reconnaissance cycle

No step was designed in isolation. Each was shaped by every other.

## STRIDe + fEAR — THE COMPLETE SYSTEM

fEAR is the sensor array. STRIDe is the locomotion.

```
fEAR:    flow → Evolution → Adversarial → Response
         (what we sense, how the case grows, what the adversary
          does, how we calibrate)

STRIDe:  Strategy → Tactics → Reconnaissance → Intelligence
         → Deployment → execution
         (where we're going, how we move, what we see, what we
          know, what we stage, and finally — the step forward)
```

The two systems operate in parallel, not in sequence. STRIDe is
not "after" fEAR. They are concurrent. While fEAR processes the
adversary's latest filing, STRIDe is already reconnoitering the
next terrain (LARA filing, LinkedIn profile, Chalkbeat archive).
While STRIDe deploys resources for Monday's route, fEAR is
evolving the case in response to what Saturday's OSINT uncovered.

The stride doesn't stop for the sensing. The sensing doesn't
pause for the stride. They are the two legs of the gait.

## THE GAIT METAPHOR

Walking is controlled falling. Each stride is a recovery from
the previous one. You lean forward, catch yourself, lean forward
again. The ground is never perfectly flat. The sidewalk cracks.
The curb rises. The terrain changes.

An efficient walker doesn't think about each step. They
internalize the process until the gait is autonomous — the
body adjusts to the terrain before the conscious mind registers
the change. The ankle flexes. The knee absorbs. The hip
compensates. The stride continues.

STRIDe is that internalization applied to operational process.
When Monday morning arrives and Kevin walks into the State Bar,
he is not thinking about execution. He is in stride — the
documents are printed (Deployment), the route is mapped
(Tactics), the network is understood (Intelligence), the
terrain was read (Reconnaissance), the three-case structure
holds (Strategy), and the foot hits the ground (execution).

The stumble is expected. The notary is closed. The MDE office
is on lunch break. The AG receptionist doesn't know where to
route the complaint. These are cracks in the sidewalk. The
stride absorbs them. You catch yourself and keep walking.

The adversary stumbles differently. They stumble because their
stride was never coherent — they improvised execution without
Strategy, deployed without Reconnaissance, and confused
Tactics with Intelligence. Banks told Kevin "I'm a lawyer"
as execution — an improvised act from a position of assumed
authority. There was no Strategy behind it, no Intelligence
informing it, no Deployment preparing for what would happen
next. It was a reflex. And the reflex created standing for
the system that will hold him accountable.

## CROSS-DOMAIN VALIDATION

STRIDe, like fEAR, is domain-independent. It applies wherever
a process must be continuous, adaptive, and self-correcting:

| Domain | S | T | R | I | D | e |
|--------|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Military operations | Mission | OPORD | Patrol/imagery | IPB/briefing | Staging/rehearsal | Execute |
| Microbiology | Experimental design | Protocol | Literature/observation | Data analysis | Media prep/mise en place | Run the assay |
| Cooking | Menu | Recipe order | Ingredients/equipment check | Understanding the science | Mise en place | Cook and plate |
| Legal (Kevin v. World) | Three-case structure | Filing sequence | OSINT | Network analysis | Print/notarize/route | Walk in, hand it over |
| ecoPrimals | Paper reproduction | Build order | Read the paper | Validate against baseline | CI/gate pipeline | `cargo test` |
| Thermal sovereignty | Livable conditions | Retrofit strategy | Building assessment | Heat transfer analysis | Material procurement | Install |

The components are universal. The instantiation is local.

## RELATIONSHIP TO K-NOME

K-NOME is the optimization framework. fEAR is the sensing
layer. STRIDe is the motion layer.

```
K-NOME (constrained optimization)
  │
  ├── fEAR (sensing + evolution)
  │     └── How the system perceives and adapts
  │
  └── STRIDe (motion + process)
        └── How the system moves and acts
```

Together, they describe a complete operational methodology:
perceive the environment (fEAR), understand it (STRIDe: R→I),
plan within it (STRIDe: S→T), prepare for action (STRIDe: D),
and act (STRIDe: e) — while continuously sensing the
adversary's response (fEAR) and adapting the stride accordingly.

The system walks. The ground changes. The system adjusts.
The adversary reacts. The system senses. The stride continues.
