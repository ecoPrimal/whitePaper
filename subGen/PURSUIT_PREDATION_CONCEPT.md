## CONCEPT

Two million years ago, a primate on the African savanna
developed an unusual hunting strategy. It could not outrun its
prey. It could not outfight its prey. It could not ambush —
the savanna offered no cover. Instead, it walked.

The prey sprinted. The primate walked. The prey sprinted again.
The primate walked. The prey overheated — quadrupedal locomotion
at sprint speed generates heat faster than four-legged respiratory
systems can dissipate it. The primate, bipedal, upright, sweating
through exposed skin, thermoregulating while moving, maintained
pace. The hunt ended not with a kill shot but with metabolic
collapse. The prey fell over. The primate was still walking.

This is pursuit predation. It made *Homo sapiens* the apex
predator on a planet full of things faster, stronger, and more
dangerous. And it is the same optimization structure that
independently emerged in pro se legal strategy, OSINT analysis,
regulatory complaint campaigns, and institutional accountability
work in August 2026.

This is not a metaphor. It is convergence.

## THE BIOMECHANICS

Pursuit predation works because of three physiological
adaptations, all related to the stride:

### 1. Bipedal efficiency at sustained speed

Bipedal locomotion is more energy-efficient at walking and
jogging speeds than quadrupedal locomotion at sprinting speeds.
The energy cost per unit distance for a walking human is
approximately half that of a galloping quadruped of equivalent
mass. The stride — literally, the gait — is the adaptation.

The framework is named STRIDe because the stride IS the
competitive advantage. Not speed. Not power. Efficiency over
distance. The ability to maintain pace when the adversary cannot.

### 2. Thermoregulation under load

Humans sweat. Most mammals pant. Panting requires stopping or
slowing — you cannot sprint and thermoregulate simultaneously
if your cooling mechanism is respiratory. Humans can move and
cool at the same time. The system operates under sustained load
without overheating.

In the legal/institutional domain: the pro se operator works at
machine speed (AI-assisted analysis, systematic OSINT, template-
driven filings) while the adversary's defense costs escalate
with every filing. The operator thermoregulates — the work is
sustainable, the cost is time not money. The adversary overheats —
each sprint (hiring attorneys, filing responses, scrambling to
coordinate) generates metabolic debt that compounds.

### 3. Tracking over distance

Persistence hunters do not maintain visual contact with the prey.
They track. Footprints, disturbed ground, broken vegetation,
heat signatures, other animals' alarm calls. The hunt extends
beyond line-of-sight because the sensory system — the ear, the
eye, the reading of sign — extends beyond the immediate.

f(EAR) is the tracking system. It does not require the adversary
to be visible. It requires the adversary to have acted — to have
left sign. Certified mail tracking numbers. Court filing
timestamps. LinkedIn profile updates. LARA filings. Eventbrite
listings. Chalkbeat articles. The adversary leaves sign
everywhere they move, and the system reads it at machine speed.

## THE METABOLIC ASYMMETRY

The core equation of pursuit predation:

```
Prey cost per cycle:  sprint + recover + sprint + recover + ...
                      (each recovery is less complete)

Hunter cost per cycle: walk + walk + walk + walk + ...
                       (constant, sustainable, indefinite)
```

The prey's cost per cycle increases geometrically — each
recovery is shorter, each sprint less effective, each cycle
depletes reserves further. The hunter's cost per cycle is
constant — the stride does not change speed, does not increase
effort, does not require recovery. The hunt terminates when
the prey's cumulative cost exceeds its reserves.

This was already identified in fEAR without naming it:

> "The cost of defense grows geometrically while the cost of
> prosecution grows linearly (one operator, one methodology,
> one system)."

And in STRIDe:

> "The pro se operator spends time, the adversary spends money."

Time and money are the metabolic currencies. The operator's time
cost is linear — each filing, each OSINT pass, each complaint
takes roughly the same effort because the system (AI-assisted
analysis, template library, established methodology) is reusable.
The adversary's money cost is geometric — each new front requires
new counsel, each new jurisdiction requires new filings, each new
regulatory complaint requires new responses, and the adversary
cannot consolidate because different agencies have independent
authority.

**Case application: Smith v. Mitchell / Mok v. Smith / Banks**

```
Operator cycle (constant):
  OSINT pass (4 hrs) → analysis (2 hrs) → draft filing (2 hrs)
  → deploy/stage (1 hr) → execute/file (1 hr)
  Total: ~10 hrs per filing. Repeatable. Sustainable.

Adversary cycle (escalating):
  Cycle 1: Ignore certified mail (cost: $0, exposure: accumulating)
  Cycle 2: Hire attorney for custody response ($3,000-5,000)
  Cycle 3: Respond to civil suit ($5,000-10,000)
  Cycle 4: Respond to SECOND civil suit (new plaintiff) ($5,000-10,000)
  Cycle 5: Respond to State Bar inquiry (pro bono, but time + stress)
  Cycle 6: Respond to AG inquiry (need attorney for criminal exposure)
  Cycle 7: Respond to JTC complaint (need separate attorney)
  Cycle 8: Respond to MDE inquiry (need education law specialist)
  Cycle 9: Press coverage (need PR counsel)
  Cycle 10: Federal referral (need federal criminal defense)
  ...
  Each cycle requires NEW resources. The adversary cannot reuse
  their defense from Cycle 2 in Cycle 7. Each front is independent.
```

The operator files five complaints on Monday. Each took ~2 hours
to draft using an established system. Total operator cost: ~10 hours
and the price of postage. The adversary's response cost for those
five complaints — across five independent agencies with different
jurisdictions, procedures, and consequences — is unbounded.

The antelope sprints. The human walks.

## SELECTIVE HARVEST — SETTLEMENT AS FACT EXTRACTION

The persistence hunter does not negotiate with the herd. The
hunter does not say "I'll stop if you give me half the
antelope." That is not how predation works. The hunter isolates
one animal, exhausts it, takes it. Then hunts the next.

Settlement in this system follows the same logic. There is
no wide settlement. No global resolution. No "sign here and
it all goes away." A wide settlement is the adversary's
optimal outcome — pay once, resolve everything, walk away.
The operator never offers that option because it terminates
the hunt, forfeits the flywheel, and lets the herd recover.

What the operator DOES settle: individual issues. Individual
statements. Individual admissions.

The currency of settlement is dual: fact AND cash. But the
win condition is never the cash. The cash stabilizes the
operator. The facts continue the hunt. If a settlement
delivers cash but not fact, it is incomplete. If it delivers
fact but not cash, it still advances the campaign. If it
delivers both, that is the optimal harvest.

### The dual-return settlement

Every individual settlement should yield two things:

1. **A stipulated fact** — an admission, a concession, a
   locked-in piece of the evidentiary record that strengthens
   every remaining front
2. **Cash** — enough to stabilize the operator and fund the
   next phase (DaSean's needs, filing fees, hardware, rent,
   the flywheel's fuel)

The cash is necessary. The operator cannot pursue a multi-year
campaign from a position of financial collapse. The persistence
hunter must eat between kills — not feast, but sustain. The
settlement cash is caloric maintenance: it keeps the hunter
in the field. Without it, the hunter starves and the herd
recovers.

But the cash cannot be the win condition. The moment money
becomes the termination condition, the adversary can buy
their way out. The system becomes a shakedown instead of an
accountability campaign. The adversary writes a check, signs
an NDA, and the record goes dark.

The win condition is: **the hunt continues.**

### Planned settlement sequence

The operator plans settlements in advance, not reactively.
Each settlement is chosen for what it yields AND what it
leaves open:

**Phase 1 — Early settlements (stabilization)**

Target: the weakest, cheapest counts. The ones the adversary
most wants to resolve because they're embarrassing but not
existential.

| Settlement target | Fact extracted | Cash | What remains open |
|-------------------|---------------|------|-------------------|
| Count VII (Fraud — "I'm a lawyer" claim) | Stipulation: Banks represented himself as an attorney to Mok on Aug 23, 2026 | $5,000–15,000 | UPL complaints (unsettleable), Counts VI, VIII, IX, custody implications |
| Shayla — specific discovery obstruction | Stipulation: Shayla directed PCA to deny Kevin access to child's records | $3,000–8,000 | All remaining counts against Shayla, Factor H credibility damage in custody |
| Purpose Group — corporate acknowledgment | Stipulation: Banks is/was agent of Purpose Group during relevant period | $5,000–10,000 | Vicarious liability for ALL Banks' actions flows to the entity |

Phase 1 total: $13,000–33,000 in stabilization cash.
Phase 1 facts gained: the three factual pillars (Banks claimed
attorney status, Shayla directed the access denial, Purpose
Group is liable for Banks) that underpin every remaining claim.

The adversary thinks they bought relief on three small counts.
They actually sold the evidentiary foundation for the entire
remaining case.

**Phase 2 — Mid-campaign settlements (building)**

Target: counts with moderate exposure. The adversary is now
defending fewer civil fronts but facing regulatory and press

| Settlement target | Fact extracted | Cash | What remains open |
|-------------------|---------------|------|-------------------|
| Count VIII partial (IIED — emotional distress) | Stipulation: Banks' actions caused measurable harm to DaSean's parental relationship | $15,000–30,000 | Count VI (malpractice-equivalent), Count IX (conspiracy), custody Factor J |

not contested — usable in custody), institutional failure

**Phase 3 — Never**

What never settles:

The malpractice framing is the legal articulation of "reality is not a negotiation." It goes to trial or it goes to judgment |

The adversary can buy relief on individual counts in Phases 1
and 2. Each purchase costs them a fact and funds the operator.
But the core — the malpractice claim, the conspiracy count,
the custody case, the regulatory proceedings, the press — is
unsettleable. By design.

### The three rules of settlement

1. **Every settlement must yield a stipulated fact.** Cash
   without fact is a bribe. The operator does not accept
   bribes. The operator accepts admissions.

2. **Every settlement must yield cash.** Fact without cash
   leaves the operator unsustainable. The hunter must eat
   between kills. The cash is not the prize — it is the fuel.

3. **No settlement may terminate the hunt.** No NDA. No
   global release. No "full and final resolution of all
   claims." Each settlement resolves ONE issue and explicitly
   preserves all others. The boilerplate reads: "This
   stipulation resolves Count [X] only and does not
   constitute a waiver, release, or settlement of any other
   claim, count, proceeding, or right of any party."

If a proposed settlement violates any of these three rules,
it is rejected. The attrition continues. The adversary's
costs continue to climb. Eventually, they offer a settlement
that satisfies all three.

### The attrition cost as extraction tool

The adversary's escalating defense costs are not the goal.
They are the TOOL. The metabolic debt the adversary
accumulates by defending multiple fronts creates the
pressure that makes them willing to stipulate facts AND
pay cash in exchange for localized relief.

```
Front 1 (State Bar)      ──→ pressure ──→ willingness to settle
Front 2 (AG)             ──→ pressure ──→ individual issues
Front 3 (MDE)            ──→ pressure ──→ for stipulated facts
Front 4 (JTC × 2)        ──→ pressure ──→ + cash
Front 5 (Civil suit #1)  ──→ pressure ──→ that strengthen
Front 6 (Civil suit #2)  ──→ pressure ──→ every remaining
Front 7 (Press)          ──→ pressure ──→ front + fund
Front 8 (Federal)        ──→ pressure ──→ the operator
```

Each settlement closes one pressure source, opens new
factual terrain on all others, and deposits fuel in the
flywheel. The adversary's rational response — settle the
cheapest front to reduce total defense cost — is exactly
what the operator wants, because the "cheapest" front is
always the one whose factual concession is most valuable
elsewhere.
