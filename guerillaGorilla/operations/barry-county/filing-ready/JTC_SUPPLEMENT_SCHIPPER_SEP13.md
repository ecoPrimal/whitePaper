## NEW EVIDENCE: ENTRY NO. 135

On September 12, 2026, independent investigative journalist
Rita Williams, editor of Clutch Justice, published documented
evidence that Docket Entry No. 135 in a Barry County matter —
an order bearing Judge Schipper's name — was not visible on the
public docket at 3:42 PM on September 11, 2026, but was present
by approximately 9:49 AM on September 12. The entry carries a
printed event date of September 10, 2026.

Ms. Williams checked the docket seven minutes after the
Michigan Supreme Court accepted her amicus curiae brief in *In
re Foley*, Michigan Supreme Court Case No. 170670, which
specifically addressed Barry County's record-keeping and
service failures. The entry was not there. Eighteen hours later,
it was — backdated to the day before her filing.

Entry No. 135 states: "RE: Defendant's Request For Status
Conference Is Dismissed; Sent to Defendant via last known
address."

A Michigan Department of Corrections FOIA response obtained by
Ms. Williams shows no record of mail from Barry County arriving
at the relevant MDOC facility on the timeline the court's own
records claim it was sent.

## THIS IS NOT AN ISOLATED INCIDENT

Ms. Williams' amicus brief in *In re Foley* (a copy of which I
have enclosed) documents a pattern of record-keeping and service
failures in Barry County that extends across multiple cases and
multiple years:

1. **Disappearing docket entries (July 2026):** In Case No.
   2022-0000000523-FH, USPS delivery confirmation (tracking nos.
   9205590324992801759012 and 9205590324992801759753) proves that
   an incarcerated litigant's motions were delivered to the Barry
   County Circuit Court on July 1, 2026. The court's docket
   initially reflected these motions as filed. On July 8, 2026 —
   the same day Ms. Williams filed correspondence with the
   Michigan Supreme Court about prison mail delivery in Case No.
   167549 — the docket entries disappeared. They did not reappear
   until August 7, 2026.

2. **False mailing claim (August 2026):** On August 11, 2026,
   the court entered an order denying the motions and represented
   in its docket that the order was mailed by first-class mail.
   MDOC's certified records production shows no record of that
   order ever reaching the defendant.

3. **Physical record tampering (February 2026):** Ms. Williams
   has separately reported a documented instance in which a
   Barry County court filing's received stamp was obscured with
   correction fluid (White-Out) before the document was returned.
   This matter is the subject of a **pending SCAO investigation**
   into the conduct of a Barry County chief probation officer.

4. **Persistent service failure (2022-2026):** In the Foley
   matter itself, the plaintiff's correct mailing address was
   placed on the court's record twice — on October 3, 2022, via
   a written communication from a deputy clerk, and on June 16,
   2023, when a FOC referee read the address into the record.
   Mail addressed to Mr. Foley continued going to the wrong
   address after each correction. Mr. Foley was subsequently
   arrested on August 17, 2026, and arraigned on August 19, on
   orders he alleges he never properly received.

## MY PROFESSIONAL ASSESSMENT

As a data scientist and laboratory scientist trained in chain of
custody protocols, I offer the following assessment:

A court's docket — the register of actions maintained under MCR
8.119 — is a forensic artifact. It is subject to the same
authentication requirements as any evidence relied upon to
determine outcomes for human beings.

In a BSL2 laboratory, if a technician claims a sample was
shipped to a receiving laboratory, and the receiving laboratory
has no record of arrival, the shipping laboratory bears the
burden of producing documentation: shipping labels, tracking
numbers, carrier confirmations. The absence of receipt is not
ambiguous — it is a chain of custody failure. The claimed
shipment is treated as unverified until the shipping
documentation is produced.

In a military intelligence context, if an analyst claims a
report was transmitted to higher command, and higher command has
no record of receipt, the analyst must produce the transmission
log. The claim alone is insufficient. A system that accepts
unverified claims of transmission is a system that cannot be
relied upon.

The same standard applies to court records. When Barry County's
docket states "Sent to Defendant via last known address," that
is a claim of fact entered into an official record. When MDOC's
certified records production shows no corresponding arrival,
the claim is unverified. When the court produces no postage
documentation, tracking numbers, or certificates of mailing,
the claim is unsupported.

An unsupported claim of fact in an official record is not an
administrative error. It is a false entry in a public record.

The pattern documented by Ms. Williams — spanning three years,
multiple cases, physical and electronic manipulation, involving
multiple judicial officers — is not consistent with isolated
human error. It is consistent with a systemic practice in which
court records are created, modified, or removed based on
factors other than what actually occurred.
