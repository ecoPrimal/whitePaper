Dear State Court Administrator:

Pursuant to MCR 8.113(A), I submit this request for
investigation of the Barry County Trial Court's compliance
with SCAO records management standards and audit requirements
as they pertain to the integrity of the court's MiCOURT
electronic case management system.

I am a Michigan citizen with a Master of Science in Data
Science from Michigan State University (2025) and five years
of BSL2 laboratory research experience involving chain of
custody protocols for scientific records. I have no personal
stake in any Barry County case.

## EXISTING SCAO INVESTIGATION

I am aware that this office initiated an investigation in
February 2026 concerning the conduct of Barry County 56B
District Court Chief Probation Officer Chrystal Lambert,
specifically the alteration of an officially stamped court
filing and its removal from the Register of Actions. That
investigation, as reported by Clutch Justice, remains pending.

The concerns I raise here are related but distinct: they
concern the integrity of Barry County's **electronic** records
in MiCOURT, not physical document tampering.

## DOCUMENTED CONCERNS

Independent investigative journalist Rita Williams has
documented the following in her amicus curiae brief filed
September 11, 2026, in *In re Foley*, Michigan Supreme Court
Case No. 170670 (enclosed):

1. **Docket entries disappeared from the Register of Actions**
   in Case No. 2022-0000000523-FH on July 8, 2026, despite
   USPS delivery confirmation proving the underlying filings
   were received by the court. The entries did not reappear
   until August 7, 2026.

2. **A docket entry (No. 135) appeared on a separate Barry
   County docket** between 3:42 PM September 11, 2026, and
   approximately 9:49 AM September 12, 2026, carrying a
   backdated event date of September 10.

3. **The court's docket claimed an order was mailed** on
   August 11, 2026. A certified MDOC FOIA response shows no
   record of that order reaching the relevant MDOC facility.

## SCAO COMPLIANCE QUESTIONS

Barry County's MiCOURT installation operates on IBM Db2, which
generates audit trail metadata automatically. SCAO's own
District Court Audit Program Template (Automated System Security
and Controls, §§ 7-9) requires:

- Reports reflecting case files that have been **modified**
- Reports reflecting case files that have been **deleted**
- Review of modification/deletion reports by an individual who
  does not have the ability to make those modifications
  (separation of duties)
- Documentation of employees who have authority to establish
  security and user classifications
- Unique passwords for each employee

**I request that SCAO determine:**

1. Whether Barry County's MiCOURT installation is generating
   the modification and deletion reports mandated by SCAO audit
   standards.

2. Whether those reports are being reviewed by an individual
   with separation of duties as required.

3. Whether the MiCOURT audit trail (Db2 audit logs) for Entry
   No. 135 shows a creation timestamp consistent with the
   printed event date of September 10, 2026.

4. Whether the Judicial Data Warehouse ingestion records for
   the relevant Barry County cases show data upload timestamps
   consistent with the claimed entry dates.

5. Whether Barry County's user access classifications and
   security controls comply with Michigan Trial Court Records
   Management Standards § 2.4.

## WHY SCAO IS UNIQUELY POSITIONED

SCAO controls infrastructure that Barry County does not:

- The **Judicial Data Warehouse** receives CMS data from Barry
  County on a daily or weekly basis. The JDW ingestion timestamp
  shows when data arrived at the state level — independent of
  Barry County's local records.

- **Network authentication logs** for Barry County's MiCOURT
  access are maintained by JIS Infrastructure, not by Barry
  County.

- **SCAO audit authority** extends to all trial courts under MCR
  8.113 and the Records Management Standards.

A SCAO-directed review of these systems would independently
verify or refute the dates on every contested docket entry
without relying on Barry County's own production.
