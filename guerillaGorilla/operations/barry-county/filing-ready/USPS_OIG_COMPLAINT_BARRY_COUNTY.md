Barry County Circuit Court has repeatedly claimed in official
court records that documents were mailed via United States
Postal Service to parties in active cases, when independent
evidence demonstrates that those mailings either never entered
the USPS mail stream or never reached their documented
destinations.

I am a Michigan citizen with a Master of Science in Data
Science from Michigan State University, five years of BSL2
laboratory experience in chain of custody protocols, and three
years of U.S. Army intelligence training in evidence
verification. I am writing because the pattern documented below
implicates the United States Postal Service in one of two ways,
and the USPS OIG has jurisdiction to determine which.

## THE PATTERN

### Incident 1: August 2026 — Court Claims Mailing, MDOC Says Nothing Arrived

On August 11, 2026, Barry County Circuit Court entered an order
in Case No. 2022-0000000523-FH denying an incarcerated
defendant's motions. The court's docket represents that this
order was mailed to the defendant by first-class mail.

A certified Michigan Department of Corrections (MDOC) records
production covering the relevant period shows NO record of that
order — or any comparable correspondence from Barry County —
ever reaching the defendant at the MDOC facility.

This MDOC FOIA response was obtained by Rita Williams, editor
of Clutch Justice (clutchjustice.com), and is referenced in her
amicus curiae brief filed with the Michigan Supreme Court in
*In re Foley*, Case No. 170670 (filed September 11, 2026).

### Incident 2: September 2026 — "Sent to Defendant" with No Proof

On or around September 10-12, 2026, Docket Entry No. 135 in a
separate Barry County matter appeared on the public docket
stating: "RE: Defendant's Request For Status Conference Is
Dismissed; Sent to Defendant via last known address."

Ms. Williams documented that this entry was NOT present on the
public docket at 3:42 PM on September 11 and WAS present by
approximately 9:49 AM on September 12. The entry carries a
printed date of September 10 — the day before Ms. Williams'
amicus brief was accepted by the Michigan Supreme Court.

The MDOC FOIA response referenced above shows no record of mail
from Barry County arriving at the relevant MDOC facility on the
timeline the court's docket claims it was sent.

### Incident 3: July 2026 — Docket Entries Disappeared After USPS Delivery Confirmed

On July 1, 2026, USPS delivered an incarcerated litigant's
motion for relief from judgment to Barry County Circuit Court.
USPS tracking number: **9205590324992801759012** (delivered
July 1, 2026). The same filings were delivered to the Barry
County Prosecutor's Office on July 2, 2026 via USPS tracking
number: **9205590324992801759753**.

Barry County Circuit Court's docket initially reflected these
motions as filed July 1, 2026. On July 8, 2026, the docket
entries for those filings **disappeared** from the public
register of actions. They did not reappear until August 7, 2026.

USPS delivery confirmation proves the court received these
documents. The court then removed them from the public record
for 30 days.

## THE TWO POSSIBILITIES

For each incident where Barry County claims to have mailed
court documents via USPS:

**(A) USPS carried the documents.** If so, USPS records should
confirm acceptance, transit, and delivery to the MDOC facility.
MDOC's certified records production shows no record of arrival.
Either USPS lost the documents (a USPS accountability issue) or
MDOC's logs are wrong (which MDOC's own FOIA response disputes).

**(B) USPS never received the documents.** If so, Barry County
Circuit Court is making false claims in official court records
that the United States Postal Service was used as the carrier
for court documents that were never deposited in the mail. This
constitutes misrepresentation of USPS involvement and potential
mail fraud under 18 USC 1341.

The USPS OIG has jurisdiction to determine which scenario is
correct by examining whether USPS has any record of carrying
mail from Barry County Circuit Court (220 W. State St.,
Hastings, MI 49058) to the relevant MDOC facility on or around:
- August 11, 2026 (Incident 1)
- September 10, 2026 (Incident 2)

Additionally, USPS tracking numbers 9205590324992801759012 and
9205590324992801759753 confirm that USPS DID carry filings TO
Barry County in July 2026 — documents that the court then
removed from its own docket. This is not a USPS failure; it is
evidence that the court manipulates records AFTER USPS proves
delivery.

## WHY THIS MATTERS BEYOND THIS COURT

I am reporting this to the USPS OIG because the integrity of
the United States Postal Service is being compromised regardless
of which scenario is correct. If courts can claim "mailed via
USPS" in official records without actually depositing documents
in the mail, USPS is being used as a shield for noncompliance.
If USPS is carrying documents that disappear before reaching
incarcerated recipients, there is a systemic failure in the
delivery chain that affects every incarcerated litigant served
by mail.

As a scientist trained in chain of custody protocols: if a
supply chain cannot prove a sample moved from Point A to Point
B, the supply chain is broken. If a laboratory claimed a sample
was shipped and the receiving lab has no record of arrival, the
claiming lab bears the burden of producing shipping
documentation — not the receiving lab. The same standard applies
here. Barry County claims USPS carried documents. USPS's own
customer (MDOC) has no record of receipt. Barry County has
produced no shipping documentation (postage receipts, tracking
numbers, certificates of mailing) to prove the documents
entered the USPS mail stream.
