### The Core Argument (Kevin's voice)

I am a Michigan citizen with a Master of Science in Data
Science from Michigan State University, a Bachelor of Science
in Microbiology, and three years of U.S. Army intelligence
service. My training encompasses chain of custody protocols
for biological samples, electronic records, and intelligence
products — three domains with a common standard: if a system
cannot prove a record is authentic, the record must be treated
as unverified.

Barry County Circuit Court's electronic case management system,
MiCOURT, operates on IBM Db2 — a database platform that
generates audit trail metadata automatically. Every entry has a
creation timestamp, a modification log, and an associated user
account. This metadata either confirms the printed date on a
docket entry or it contradicts it. There is no third option.

Independent investigative journalist Rita Williams has
documented five incidents spanning three years in which Barry
County's court records were manipulated: docket entries that
disappeared after USPS confirmed their delivery, entries that
appeared overnight and were backdated, mail that was claimed to
be sent but never arrived at the destination (confirmed by MDOC
FOIA), and a physical court stamp that was destroyed with
correction fluid by a court officer now under SCAO
investigation.

In the laboratory, if a technician reports a result without
chain of custody documentation, the result is rejected. In
military intelligence, if an analyst claims a transmission
occurred without a log entry, the claim is rejected. In data
science, if a database record has no audit trail, the record
is unauthenticated. Courts are not exempt from this standard.
A court record is a forensic artifact. If the metadata does
not support the printed date, the printed date is contaminated.

I have no personal stake in any Barry County case. I am not
affiliated with Ms. Williams or with any party in the matters
she has documented. I am filing independently because the
documented pattern raises questions that my professional
training qualifies me to assess, and because the integrity of
Michigan's court records affects every citizen of this state.
