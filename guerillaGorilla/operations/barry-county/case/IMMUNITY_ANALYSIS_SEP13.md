## THE RULE

Judicial immunity protects judges from civil liability for
**judicial acts** — decisions made from the bench in cases
within their jurisdiction. *Stump v. Sparkman*, 435 U.S. 349
(1978).

It does NOT protect:
- **Administrative acts** — *Forrester v. White*, 484 U.S. 219
  (1988) (firing a probation officer was administrative, not
  judicial — no immunity)
- **Ministerial acts** — maintaining records, processing mail,
  filing documents
- **Criminal conduct** — immunity is a civil defense; it does
  not shield against criminal prosecution
- **Acts in the clear absence of all jurisdiction** —
  *Mireles v. Waco*, 502 U.S. 9 (1991)
- **Conspiracy with non-judicial actors** — when a judge
  conspires with a private party, immunity can be pierced
  under 42 USC 1983

**Strategy:** Frame every complaint around actions that fall
OUTSIDE judicial immunity. Don't attack rulings. Attack record
handling, mail practices, staff supervision, technology
governance, and coordination with opposing counsel.

## FRAMING FOR EACH AUTHORITY

### FBI (MichiganCorruption@fbi.gov)
**Frame:** Criminal conduct — 18 USC 241/242/1341/1343/1346
- Judicial immunity is IRRELEVANT to criminal prosecution
- No judge is immune from federal criminal prosecution for
  wire fraud, mail fraud, conspiracy against rights, or
  deprivation of rights under color of law
- **Every action is targetable** — bench or non-bench doesn't
  matter in the criminal context

### USPS OIG + USPIS
**Frame:** Mailing practices — purely ministerial
- "Sent to Defendant via last known address" is a ministerial
  claim about a ministerial act (mailing a document)
- No judicial decision-making is involved in putting a document
  in an envelope and depositing it with USPS
- The court's mailing practices are not protected by any form
  of immunity

### JTC
**Frame:** Misconduct encompasses non-bench conduct
- MCR 9.202 defines "misconduct" broadly — includes failure
  to perform judicial duties, conduct prejudicial to the
  administration of justice, and persistent failure to
  perform duties
- Administrative failures (supervision, mail, records) are
  expressly within JTC jurisdiction
- JTC already found Schipper committed misconduct for public
  comments (2014) — a non-bench act

### SCAO (MCR 8.113)
**Frame:** Court administration — entirely non-bench
- MCR 8.113 investigates how courts OPERATE, not how judges
  RULE
- Record integrity, user access controls, SCAO compliance,
  audit trail maintenance — all administrative
- This is the cleanest track: pure administration, zero
  judicial immunity issues

### Michigan AG
**Frame:** Criminal prosecution — immunity irrelevant
- MCL 750.248 (false public record) — creating/allowing false
  entries in the register of actions
- MCL 750.423 (perjury) — false statements in official records
- No immunity defense against state criminal prosecution
- AG can investigate and prosecute without any immunity barrier

### 42 USC 1983 (Civil — if any party brings suit)
**Frame:** Target ONLY non-bench actions
- Administrative: record manipulation, mail handling, staff
  management, technology governance, supervision failures
- Ministerial: mailing documents, maintaining docket entries,
  processing filings
- Conspiracy: coordination with Brad Gee to weaponize
  vexatious order against AGC complaint (*Dennis v. Sparks*)
- **Do NOT frame as challenging judicial decisions** — the
  disqualification denial, the subpoena denial, the order
  itself are all protected. The MAILING, the RECORD-KEEPING,
  the STAFF DIRECTION, and the COORDINATION WITH OPPOSING
  COUNSEL are not.
