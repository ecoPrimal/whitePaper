## FEDERAL CIVIL RIGHTS VIOLATIONS

### 42 USC 1983 — Civil Rights Violation Under Color of Law

Every person who, under color of any statute, ordinance,
regulation, custom, or usage, of any State, subjects any citizen
to the deprivation of any rights secured by the Constitution and
laws, shall be liable.

**Application:**
- Barry County judges and court officers acted under color of
  state law (judicial authority, court administration)
- They deprived Foley of: due process (14th Amendment), right to
  petition for redress (1st Amendment), access to courts (14th
  Amendment)
- They deprived Rita of: freedom of the press (1st Amendment),
  right to petition (1st Amendment)
- The deprivation was not incidental — it was retaliatory and
  escalating

### 18 USC 241 — Conspiracy Against Rights

If two or more persons conspire to injure, oppress, threaten,
or intimidate any person in the free exercise of any right or
privilege secured by the Constitution or laws of the United
States...

**Application:**
- The suppression architecture requires coordination: judge
  issues vexatious order, opposing counsel uses it to kill AGC
  complaint, court staff directed to stop responding, probation
  officer alters records
- This is not one person acting alone — it is a coordinated
  institutional response involving Doherty, Alspaugh, Gee,
  Lambert, and whoever created/modified Entry 135
- The conspiracy targeted Foley's right to petition for redress
  and Rita's right to a free press

### 18 USC 242 — Deprivation of Rights Under Color of Law

Whoever, under color of any law, willfully subjects any person
to the deprivation of any rights secured by the Constitution...

**Application:**
- Judges acting under color of law
- Willful: the pattern of escalation shows intentional conduct
  responsive to oversight actions
- Rights deprived: due process, access to courts, petition,
  press freedom
- **Foley was IMPRISONED** — the statute provides enhanced
  penalties when deprivation results in bodily restraint
