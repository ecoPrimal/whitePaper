## Purpose

gen5 is external science production. This directory tracks each collaborator relationship as a science production instance — from first contact through engaged collaboration to published results that feed back into the ecosystem.

Every entry follows the same structure: who they are, what biological question they bring, what products compose for their work, what deliverables have been produced, and what new spring validation targets their science creates.

---

## Collaborator Status Model

| Status | Definition |
|--------|------------|
| **theory** | Collaboration is conceptualized but no contact made |
| **contacted** | Email sent, pseudoSpore or lithoSpore shared, no response or early response |
| **engaged** | Active conversation, meeting scheduled or held, project scoped |
| **producing** | Computational work underway, artifacts being generated |
| **published** | Science delivered — pseudoSpore, grant preliminary data, or paper |

## The Feedback Loop

Each collaborator's science creates new validation targets:

```
Collaborator brings question + domain data
    → Products compose for their domain
    → pseudoSpore produced (self-verifying artifact)
    → Collaborator owns the science
    → Results become new spring validation targets
    → Springs evolve from external demand
    → Products strengthen
    → Next collaborator benefits
```

The NF project illustrates this concretely: Andrea's NF gene expression results will become wetSpring validation checks. Her drug repurposing scores will become healthSpring validation checks. The ecosystem gains validation targets it could never have generated internally.

## What Makes This Different From gen4 Contacts

gen4 contacts (in `attsi/non-anon/contact/`) track the relationship: emails, interview notes, strategy. gen5 collaborator docs track the **science production instance**: what question, what products, what deliverables, what feedback. The contact files are the human story. The collaborator files are the ecosystem story.

---

*gen5 collaborators are not users. They are not customers. They are scientists whose work the ecosystem serves. The ecosystem succeeds when their science succeeds — and their success makes the ecosystem stronger.*
