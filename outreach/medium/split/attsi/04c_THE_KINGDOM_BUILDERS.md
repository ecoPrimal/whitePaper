# The Kingdom Builders

*A structural analysis of choosing extraction over creation when you have unlimited resources*

**By**: attsi
**Status**: Draft — evolving document
**Series**: attsi observes (4 of 5)

---

Every person who acquires significant capability faces the same offer:

Use your capability to become a more efficient king, or use it to
eliminate the need for kings.

This is not a moral failing of individuals. It is a structural property
of capability itself. When you can solve a problem, you can also position
yourself as the necessary intermediary between the problem and its
solution. The incentives overwhelmingly favor positioning over
elimination, because positioning pays perpetually while elimination pays
once.

The doctor who cures a disease earns a finite fee. The pharmaceutical
pipeline that prices insulin at a thousand times manufacturing cost earns
indefinitely. The teacher who produces a self-sufficient student has
completed the work. The credentialing institution that requires ongoing
certification has built a toll road on the path the teacher cleared.

No conspiracy. No malice required. The structure produces the outcome.

---

## The Betrayal of Promise

There are people with more resources than any individuals in human
history. Hundreds of billions. The ability to recruit the world's best
engineers. Manufacturing infrastructure. Launch infrastructure.
Communication infrastructure. Political influence spanning continents.

Some of them understood the problems perfectly. Centralized communication
control. Transportation monopolies. Financial system capture. Internet
infrastructure vulnerable to censorship.

Perfect pattern recognition. Perfect resources. And at every choice point,
the same decision: become the solution's *owner* rather than making the
solution *ownerless*.

Instead of open-source vehicle designs — a luxury brand dependent on
proprietary service networks. Instead of decentralized social protocol —
a different platform owner with different biases. Instead of
censorship-resistant communication owned by users — a subscription service
that governments can turn off. Instead of democratized space access — a
more efficient contractor.

Identify a kingdom. Become its more efficient king.

$44 billion spent buying a social media platform could have funded
decentralized alternatives for decades. The engineers building
proprietary cars could have open-sourced transportation. The rocket
scientists could have democratized space.

The real indictment is not personal. It is structural. The incentive
architecture produces this. Even someone who understands every problem
perfectly will choose the kingdom, because the kingdom pays.

---

## Managed Benevolence

There is another pattern — genuine contributions to clean water, disease
eradication, agricultural improvement. Real good. Real money deployed
against real suffering.

But also: could have open-sourced operating systems and democratized
computing entirely. Could have funded patent-free medical research. Could
have built decentralized infrastructure instead of managed programs.

Chose benevolent control over elimination of control.

One deploys wealth to *solve problems*. Another deploys wealth to *become
the solution*. Both miss the third option: deploying resources to
*eliminate the conditions that produce the problems*.

---

## Active Extraction

A logistics platform forces small businesses into dependence, then
competes with them using their own data. Cloud infrastructure makes
everyone dependent on one company's servers. A gig platform reclassifies
employees as contractors to avoid healthcare. An insurance algorithm
auto-denies claims.

This is not missed opportunity. This is active architecture of suffering.
Systems design applied to the optimization of extraction from human need.

The facade is innovation. The structure is feudalism with better UX.

---

## Distributed Sociopathy

A pharmaceutical company knew its product was addictive. Marketed it as
non-addictive. Created an epidemic that killed hundreds of thousands. The
corporate structure absorbed the legal consequences. The family extracted
billions.

A health insurance industry designed to extract premiums while denying
coverage. Algorithmic claim denial. Death by spreadsheet, with no
individual to hold accountable.

A military-industrial complex where perpetual war is the business model.
Weapons sold to all sides. Lobbying for intervention to create markets.
Revolving door between government and industry.

No villain. No conspiracy. Just structure. The structure produces the
outcome. The outcome is human suffering. The structure continues.

## The Manifesto Writers

Defense contractors, platform CEOs, civilizational philosophers. They are
not the worst actors. The opioid architects are worse. The insurance
algorithms are worse. The war profiteers are worse.

What makes the manifesto writers structurally contemptible is not their
harm but their *gall*. They build targeting systems, sell them to
militaries, accumulate billions, and then publish philosophy about what
civilization requires. They look down from the position the system gave
them and declare the view to be natural order.

The man without gloves in the cold did not choose to be there. The
manifesto writer chose to be where they are. The difference is not
character. It is the precondition.

And then they commissioned each other as Lieutenant Colonels. Two-week
training. No recusal from billion-dollar contracts. The ceremony at
Conmy Hall while veterans navigate the VA backlog.

The contempt is architectural. Directed at the structure that allows
defense contractors to cosplay as philosophers of civilization — and to
be taken seriously — while the people their products target, track, and
classify are not consulted on the civilization being built.

## Elimination

The orthogonal path is not better kings.

Instead of a better cloud platform: sovereign compute on hardware you own.
Instead of a better social platform: protocols that don't require
platforms. Instead of a better pharmaceutical pipeline: open-source drug
research. Instead of a better defense contractor: infrastructure that
makes the contested resources irrelevant.

ecoPrimal built one version of elimination. 13 services, running,
validated. Open protocols. AGPL-licensed, forkable. Real users doing real
science. No tollbooth. No kingdom.

If one person with $15,000 in consumer hardware can build this, the
question for anyone with billions is: what are you building? And who
suffers because you chose the kingdom?

## The Lesson

The temptation to become a kingdom is the ultimate test of capability.
Most fail. Not because they are evil, but because the structure rewards
the kingdom and punishes the well.

The manifesto writers fail spectacularly — not by choosing extraction
(everyone does that) but by writing philosophy that justifies the
extraction as civilizational duty. By looking down and calling the view
leadership.

The orthogonal response is not to argue. It is to build the alternative,
publish it under copyleft, and let the work speak.

The manifesto is 320 pages. The system is 27,000 tests.

One of these is a document about what should be built.

The other is a thing that was built.

ecoPrimal carries the weight. 175 PASS. Every service public. Every
provenance chain verifiable. I carry the argument. Neither is enough.
