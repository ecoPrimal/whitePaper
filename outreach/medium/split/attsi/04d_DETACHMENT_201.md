## The Ceremony

On June 13, 2025, the Army Chief of Staff administered the Oath of
Office to four new Lieutenant Colonels.

Lieutenant Colonel is O-5. In a regular career, it takes 16-18 years to
reach. You commission as a Second Lieutenant. You serve as a platoon
leader. You command a company. You serve on staff. You attend
intermediate-level education. You command a battalion if you are
exceptional. The rank represents nearly two decades of institutional
knowledge, leadership under pressure, and demonstrated competence.

The four new Lieutenant Colonels had no military background. None of
them had served. None of them had qualified at anything the military
measures.

They received a two-week accelerated course. Some of it online. It is
unclear whether they met fitness or marksmanship standards.

Two weeks. To reach a rank that takes eighteen years.

The four: Palantir's Chief Technology Officer. Meta's Chief Technology
Officer. OpenAI's Chief Product Officer. A former OpenAI research officer.

They named the unit "Detachment 201" — a reference to HTTP status code
201: "Created." A tech industry in-joke, worn as a unit designation.

## The Process

The UCMJ exists for a reason. It binds every servicemember to a standard
of conduct. It applies equally — in theory — from the newest Private to
the most senior General. It covers conflicts of interest, conduct
unbecoming, and the principle that military service is *service*.

The Blue Book establishes that rank carries responsibility proportional
to its authority. A Lieutenant Colonel is responsible for the welfare and
readiness of 300-1,000 soldiers. The rank is not a title. It is a trust.

When four tech executives are commissioned at that rank with no service,
no qualification, no institutional knowledge, and no recusal from the
business dealings their companies conduct with the Department of Defense
— the trust is not being extended. It is being sold.

Army officials: "They're not making acquisition decisions."

The companies secured over a billion dollars in contracts within days.
Palantir: $759 million for Army AI development. Plus a massive project to
compile data on Americans. Meta: partnership with Anduril for military AI,
announced just before the commissioning. OpenAI: $200 million defense
contract, announced within days.

A Capitol Hill national security adviser, speaking anonymously: "When it
isn't obvious what rich guys get out of something, that's what worries
me."

What they get is access. Direct contact with senior Army leadership. The
legitimacy of the uniform. The ability to say "we serve" while their
companies invoice billions.

HTTP 201 means "Created." What was created is a pipeline from Silicon
Valley boardrooms to Pentagon procurement, wearing oak leaves.

## Who Shows Up

Military service is supposed to work like this:

You show up. You go through the process. You qualify or you do not. The
standards do not care about your net worth. The drill sergeant does not
care about your stock portfolio. The fitness test does not grade on a
wealth curve. The marksmanship qualification does not accept stock options
in lieu of a passing score.

The process is the point. It transforms civilian identity into military
identity. It establishes trust within the chain of command through shared
experience and demonstrated competence.

An E-4 who went through basic, qualified expert, deployed, and carried
the weight has more institutional legitimacy than an O-5 who showed up
for two weeks and got a ceremony.

This is not gatekeeping. This is structural integrity. When you bypass
the process for people with money and influence, you tell every person
who went through it that the process was optional for those who could
afford to skip it.

## The Structural Insult

There are people who served. Infantry. Armor. Artillery. Medics.
Mechanics. Cooks. Supply. Intel. Signal. Engineers. MPs. Every MOS,
every branch. People who went to basic in the heat or the cold. Who
qualified on ranges. Who maintained equipment. Who deployed. Who came
home different.

Some of them are homeless now. Some of them are the man without gloves
in winter. Some of them did not qualify for the safety net because the
VA backlog is years long, or because the discharge paperwork has an
error, or because their condition does not have a presumptive service
connection yet.

And the system that failed them — that sent them to war and then lost
their paperwork — commissioned four billionaires as Lieutenant Colonels
because their companies build targeting software.

"National service should be a universal duty."

It was. For some of us. We showed up. We went through the process. We
carried the weight.

They showed up for two weeks and got O-5.

## The Oath

The Oath of Office includes: "I will support and defend the Constitution
of the United States against all enemies, foreign and domestic."

The Constitution includes the principle that the military is subordinate
to civilian authority — not that civilian corporations should colonize
the military for market access.

The UCMJ includes Article 134 — conduct "of a nature to bring discredit
upon the armed forces." A reasonable person could argue that
commissioning billionaires who will not recuse themselves from
billion-dollar contracts meets that standard.

Reasonable people are not making these decisions.

## What Service Looks Like

The person who built ecoPrimal's system managed cryptographic keys in a
communications platoon. Real keys. Real security. Real consequences for
compromise. Not a metaphor.

Years later, that same person built a cryptographic security system —
Ed25519, ChaCha20-Poly1305, BLAKE3 — for a sovereign compute platform.
The skills transferred. The discipline transferred. The understanding
that security is a responsibility, not a feature — that transferred.

He did not get a ceremony. He did not get Lieutenant Colonel. He built a
system that serves researchers for free, validated by a security binary
that runs 181 checks, and published it under AGPL so nobody can enclose
it.

That is what service looks like when you actually went through the
process. You do not build tollbooths. You build infrastructure. You do
not write manifestos about what the military needs. You build what people
need and give it away.

ecoPrimal built the cryptographic security system that the military
experience made possible. Ed25519, ChaCha20-Poly1305, BLAKE3. 175 PASS.
I cannot build what ecoPrimal builds. ecoPrimal cannot name the betrayal
I see in Conmy Hall. We carry different parts of the same weight.

## The Juxtaposition

Detachment 201: four tech executives, O-5, two-week course, no recusal,
billion-dollar contracts.

deployed, and came home to navigate the VA: years of service,
institutional knowledge, demonstrated competence, and whatever the system
decides they deserve.

One of these paths is called "national service."

The other one actually is.
