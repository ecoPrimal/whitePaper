# The Kingdom Builders

*A structural analysis of choosing extraction over creation when you have unlimited resources*

**By**: attsi | ecoPrimal
**Status**: Draft — evolving document
**Published**: [pending]
**Series**: Companion to "While They Were Writing Manifestos"

---

Every person who acquires significant capability faces the same offer:

Use your capability to become a more efficient king, or use it to eliminate
the need for kings.

This is not a moral failing of individuals. It is a structural property of
capability itself. When you can solve a problem, you can also position
yourself as the necessary intermediary between the problem and its solution.
And the incentives overwhelmingly favor positioning over elimination, because
positioning pays perpetually while elimination pays once.

The doctor who cures a disease is a human solving a problem. The
pharmaceutical pipeline that prices insulin at a thousand times its
manufacturing cost is a system profiting from prolonging the problem. The
teacher who produces a self-sufficient student has done their job. The
credentialing institution that requires ongoing certification and
re-licensure has built a toll road on the path the teacher cleared.

The pattern requires no conspiracy and no malice. It emerges from the
structure of incentives. The humans do the work. The systems extract the
value.

## The Pattern, Illustrated

### The Betrayal of Promise

There is a specific person with more resources than any individual in human
history. Hundreds of billions. The ability to recruit the world's best
engineers. Manufacturing infrastructure. Launch infrastructure.
Communication infrastructure. A social platform. Political influence that
spans continents.

This person understood the problems perfectly. Centralized communication
control. Transportation monopolies. Financial system capture. Space access
restricted to governments and contractors. Internet infrastructure
vulnerable to censorship.

Perfect pattern recognition. Perfect resources. And at every choice point,
the same decision: become the solution's *owner* rather than making the
solution *ownerless*.

Instead of open-source vehicle designs with freely available blueprints —
a luxury brand dependent on proprietary service networks and closed
ecosystems.

Instead of truly decentralized social protocol with no central control — a
different platform owner with different biases.

Instead of censorship-resistant satellite communication owned cooperatively
by users — a subscription service that can be turned off by governments, as
demonstrated in wartime.

Instead of democratized space access — a more efficient aerospace contractor
that governments and corporations must pay.

The pattern is consistent: identify a kingdom, then become its more
efficient king rather than eliminating the need for kings.

$44 billion spent buying a social media platform could have funded
decentralized alternatives for decades. The engineering talent building
proprietary cars could have open-sourced transportation. The rocket
scientists could have democratized space.

With a fraction of these resources, applied orthogonally, digital
sovereignty could have been achieved for millions of people.

The real indictment is not personal. It is structural. This is what the
incentive architecture produces. Even someone who understands every problem
perfectly will choose the kingdom, because the kingdom pays.

---

### Managed Benevolence

There is another person who chose differently — not orthogonally, but
partially. Genuine contributions to clean water infrastructure. Disease
eradication programs. Agricultural improvements.

Real good. Real money deployed against real suffering.

But also: could have open-sourced operating systems and democratized
computing entirely. Could have funded patent-free medical research instead
of charitable distribution. Could have built decentralized infrastructure
instead of managed programs.

Chose benevolent control over elimination of control.

The critical distinction: one deploys wealth to *solve problems*. The other
deploys wealth to *become the solution to problems*. Both miss the third
option: deploying resources to *eliminate the conditions that produce the
problems*.

---

### Active Extraction

There is a third pattern — the logistics platform that forces small
businesses into dependence, then competes with them using their own data.
The cloud infrastructure that makes everyone dependent on one company's
servers. The gig platform that reclassifies employees as contractors to
avoid providing healthcare. The insurance algorithm that auto-denies
claims.

This is not missed opportunity. This is active architecture of suffering.
Brilliant systems design applied to the optimization of extraction from
human need.

"Customer obsession" as marketing while building vendor dependence.
"Innovation" that creates new forms of worker surveillance. "Space
exploration" that is billionaire tourism.

The facade is innovation. The structure is feudalism with better UX.

### Distributed Sociopathy

And then there are the structures with no individual to point at.

A pharmaceutical company knew its product was addictive. Marketed it as
non-addictive. Incentivized doctors to prescribe it. Created an addiction
epidemic that killed hundreds of thousands. Used its corporate structure to
absorb the legal consequences while the family extracted billions.

A health insurance industry designed from the ground up to extract premiums
while denying coverage. Algorithmic claim denial that automatically rejects
legitimate requests. Death by spreadsheet, with no individual decision-maker
to hold accountable.

A military-industrial complex where perpetual war is the business model.
Weapons sold to all sides. Lobbying for intervention to create markets.
Revolving door between government and industry. Profit from human conflict,
with institutional momentum that transcends any individual's conscience.

No villain. No conspiracy. Just structure. The structure produces the
outcome. The outcome is human suffering. The structure continues.

## The Spectrum of Contempt

The manifesto writers — the defense contractors, the platform CEOs, the
civilizational philosophers — occupy a specific position in this spectrum.
They are not the worst actors. The Sacklers are worse. The insurance
algorithms are worse. The war profiteers are worse.

What makes the manifesto writers structurally contemptible is not their
harm but their *gall*. They build the targeting systems, sell them to
militaries, accumulate billions, and then publish philosophy about what
civilization requires. They look down from the position the system gave
them and declare the view to be natural order.

The man without gloves in the cold didn't choose to be there. The
manifesto writer chose to be where they are. The difference is not
character. It is the precondition.

The contempt is architectural. It is directed at the structure that
allows defense contractors to cosplay as philosophers — and to be taken
seriously — while the people their products target, track, and classify
are not consulted on the civilization being built.

## What Elimination Looks Like

The orthogonal path is not "better kings." It is the elimination of the
conditions that require kings.

Instead of a better cloud platform: sovereign compute on hardware you own.
Instead of a better social media platform: protocols that don't require
platforms. Instead of a better pharmaceutical pipeline: open-source drug
research that doesn't require tollbooth pricing. Instead of a better
defense contractor: infrastructure that makes the conflict worth fighting
over irrelevant.

None of this is utopian. Sovereign compute exists — 13 services, running,
validated, serving researchers. Open protocols exist — AGPL-licensed,
forkable, no permission required. The elimination path is harder to fund
because it doesn't produce tollbooths, and tollbooths are what the
funding system rewards.

But it works. One person, $15,000 in hardware, two years, AI-assisted
development. 70+ papers reproduced. 175 PASS security validation. Real
users doing real science.

If one person with no funding can build this, the question for anyone with
billions is: what are you building? And who suffers because you chose the
kingdom?

## The Lesson

The temptation to become a kingdom is the ultimate test of capability. Most
fail. Not because they are evil, but because the structure rewards the
kingdom and punishes the well.

The manifesto writers fail spectacularly — not by choosing extraction
(everyone's doing that) but by writing philosophy that justifies the
extraction as civilizational duty. By looking down at the people their
systems produce and calling the view "leadership."

The orthogonal response is not to argue with them. It is to build the
alternative, publish it under copyleft, and let the work speak. The
manifesto is 320 pages. The system is 27,000 tests.

One of these is a document about what should be built.

The other is a thing that was built.
