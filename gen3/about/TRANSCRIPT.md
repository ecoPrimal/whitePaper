## Relevant Coursework for ecoPrimals

### Microbiology (BS) - The Biological Foundation

The constrained evolution methodology (`CONSTRAINED_EVOLUTION_FORMAL.md`) and the enzyme thesis (`P_NP_ENZYME_THESIS.md`) draw directly from:

- **MMG 431 - Microbial Genetics**: Gene regulation, mutation, horizontal gene transfer, genetic analysis of bacterial populations. This is the course where Lenski's LTEE became personally meaningful - not as a textbook example, but as a model for how populations evolve under constraint.
- **MMG 421 - Prokaryotic Cell Physiology**: Bacterial metabolism, enzyme kinetics, membrane transport, energy production. The foundation for understanding Taq polymerase as a constraint-specific adaptation and enzymes as generative solutions to chemical NP problems.
- **MMG 413 - Virology**: Viral replication strategies, host-pathogen interaction, genetic variation mechanisms. Genetic diversity as a survival strategy.
- **MMG 461 - Molecular Pathogenesis**: Virulence factor regulation, signal transduction in bacteria, immune evasion. How organisms adapt to hostile environments through selective pressure.
- **MMG 365 - Medical Microbiology**: Clinical microbiology, selective media, differential diagnosis. The practical art of identifying bacteria by their environment-specific traits - fastidiousness as diagnostic criterion.
- **IBIO 341 - Fundamental Genetics**: Classical and molecular genetics. The genome as information storage, mutation as search, selection as verification.
- **BMB 401 - Comprehensive Biochemistry**: Enzyme kinetics, protein structure, metabolic pathways. The chemistry behind the enzyme thesis.

### Data Science (MS) - The Computational Framework

The computational side of the constrained evolution methodology draws from:

- **CMSE 831 - Computational Optimization**: Constrained optimization theory, gradient methods, convergence analysis. The formal mathematical framework that the biological analogy extends.
- **CSE 847 - Machine Learning**: Learning theory, generalization, bias-variance tradeoff. AI as a search process in a solution space.
- **CSE 849 - Deep Learning**: Neural architectures, training dynamics, loss landscapes. Computational analogies to fitness landscapes.
- **CSE 840 - Computational Foundations in AI**: Search algorithms, heuristics, NP-completeness. The formal background for the P != NP enzyme thesis.
- **CSE 830 - Design & Theory of Algorithms**: Algorithmic complexity, NP-completeness proofs, approximation algorithms. Direct foundation for the P != NP argument.
- **CMSE 890 - Applied Machine Learning** (Professor Murillo): Where reservoir computing, echo state machines, and neuromorphic hardware were first discussed - leading directly to the BrainChip Akida work in ToadStool.
