# Model Ordinance: Native Plant Protection for the City of Lansing

**Purpose:** Draft model ordinance protecting native Michigan plantings
from aesthetic enforcement on easements and residential properties.
For introduction to Lansing City Council after administrative appeal
or litigation establishes precedent.

**Model precedent:** Maryland HB 322 (2021) — preempted HOA restrictions
on native plants. Virginia SB 522 (2021) — "Virginia's Right to Grow
Native Plants" act. Multiple municipalities have adopted pollinator-
friendly vegetation standards.

**Author:** Kevin Mok (BS Microbiology, MSU; MS Data Science, MSU)
**Date:** August 25, 2026

## PROPOSED ORDINANCE

### Chapter ___: NATIVE VEGETATION PROTECTION

#### Section 1. Purpose and Findings

The City Council of the City of Lansing finds that:

(a) Native Michigan plant species provide critical ecological services
including pollinator habitat, stormwater management, soil health
maintenance, carbon sequestration, and wildlife support.

(b) The Michigan Pollinator Protection Plan (2019), published by the
Michigan Department of Agriculture and Rural Development, recognizes
the importance of native plantings for pollinator conservation.

(c) Native plantings reduce municipal stormwater infrastructure costs
by absorbing rainfall through deep root systems, consistent with the
City's MS4 stormwater permit obligations.

(d) Enforcement of vegetation height or aesthetic standards against
native Michigan species, when those species do not interfere with
utility function or public safety, discourages ecologically beneficial
land management practices.

(e) The purpose of this ordinance is to protect the right of property
owners to cultivate native Michigan plant species on their property,
including within utility easements and rights-of-way, provided such
plantings do not interfere with the designated function of the
easement or right-of-way.

#### Section 2. Definitions

(a) **"Native Michigan plant"** means any plant species indigenous to
the state of Michigan as documented by the Michigan Natural Features
Inventory (MNFI), the USDA PLANTS Database, or Michigan Flora Online.

(b) **"Noxious weed"** means any species designated as a noxious weed
under the Michigan Noxious Weed Act (MCL 247.61-71) or by the Michigan
Department of Agriculture and Rural Development.

(c) **"Managed native planting"** means a deliberate planting of native
Michigan species maintained by the property owner, distinguishable from
neglected or abandoned vegetation by the presence of: defined borders,
species diversity, evidence of intentional placement, or identification
signage.

(d) **"Easement function"** means the specific purpose for which a
utility, drainage, or right-of-way easement was established, including
but not limited to: access to utility infrastructure, public passage,
stormwater conveyance, or sight-line maintenance for traffic safety.

#### Section 3. Protection of Native Plantings

(a) No citation, fine, or enforcement action shall be issued against a
property owner for the cultivation of native Michigan plants on the
basis of height, density, or aesthetic appearance, unless the enforcing
authority can demonstrate that the specific planting:

  (1) Physically obstructs access to utility infrastructure;
  (2) Creates a documented public safety hazard (sight-line obstruction,
      fire hazard, or similar);
  (3) Constitutes a noxious weed as defined in Section 2(b); or
  (4) Interferes with the designated function of a public right-of-way.

(b) The burden of demonstrating interference under subsection (a) shall
rest with the enforcing authority, not the property owner.

(c) Aesthetic objections, including but not limited to height exceeding
a numerical standard, "untidiness," or nonconformity with surrounding
turf lawns, shall not constitute grounds for enforcement against native
Michigan plantings.

#### Section 4. Identification Signage (Optional Safe Harbor)

(a) A property owner may, but is not required to, post a sign
identifying a planting as a "Native Michigan Plant Garden" or
"Pollinator Habitat."

(b) The presence of such signage shall create a rebuttable presumption
that the planting is a managed native planting under Section 2(c) and
entitled to the protections of Section 3.

(c) The City shall make available, at no cost, standardized signage
templates for native plant garden identification.

#### Section 5. Managed Native Planting Standards

(a) To qualify for the protections of Section 3, a managed native
planting should include at least three (3) native Michigan species.

(b) A managed native planting shall maintain a defined border or edge
separating it from adjacent turf, sidewalk, or roadway. Borders may
include but are not limited to: mowed strips, edging material, stone,
or mulch.

(c) A property owner maintaining a managed native planting on a utility
easement shall allow reasonable access for utility maintenance upon
request by the easement holder.

#### Section 6. Enforcement Procedures

(a) Before issuing any citation involving vegetation that may include
native Michigan species, the enforcing officer shall:

  (1) Attempt to identify the species present;
  (2) Consult the USDA PLANTS Database or Michigan Flora Online to
      determine native status;
  (3) Document the specific easement function interference, if any;
  (4) Provide the property owner a written notice specifying the
      interference and a 30-day cure period before any fine is imposed.

(b) If the property owner disputes the citation and demonstrates that
the vegetation consists of native Michigan species, the citation shall
be withdrawn unless the enforcing authority provides written
documentation of specific interference under Section 3(a).

#### Section 7. Existing Ordinance Harmonization

(a) To the extent that any existing City of Lansing ordinance conflicts
with this chapter, this chapter shall control with respect to native
Michigan plant species.

(b) This ordinance does not affect enforcement against noxious weeds
as defined under MCL 247.61-71.

(c) This ordinance does not affect the authority of utility easement
holders to maintain their infrastructure, provided such maintenance
is limited to the area immediately surrounding the infrastructure.

#### Section 8. Severability

If any section, subsection, or provision of this ordinance is held
invalid by a court of competent jurisdiction, such invalidity shall not
affect the remaining provisions.

#### Section 9. Effective Date

This ordinance shall take effect thirty (30) days after adoption.

## Supporting Evidence for Council Presentation

### Ecological

- Michigan Pollinator Protection Plan (MDARD, 2019)
- USDA PLANTS Database native species listings
- Michigan Natural Features Inventory
- ecoPrimals baseCamp Papers: 03 (Precision Microbiome), 04 (Microbial
  Sentinels), 06 (No-Till Anderson), 08 (NPU Agricultural IoT)
- Deep root systems reduce stormwater runoff 40-60% vs. turf (USEPA)

### Legal

- Maryland HB 322 (2021): native plant HOA preemption
- Virginia SB 522 (2021): right to grow native plants
- Michigan Right to Farm Act (MCL 286.471-474)
- Michigan NREPA (MCL 324.101 et seq.)
- Michigan Noxious Weed Act (MCL 247.61-71)
- Michigan Pollinator Protection Plan (state policy alignment)

### Economic

- Native plantings reduce municipal mowing costs
- Stormwater management savings ($0.10-1.50/gal retained)
- Pollinator habitat supports $104B Michigan agricultural economy
- Property value studies: managed native gardens maintain or increase
  values (University of Michigan Extension, multiple studies)
