## Phase 2: Numeric Space (N) — Legal Research Targets

### Michigan-specific defenses:

1. **Michigan Right to Farm Act (RTFA)** — MCL 286.471-474
   - Protects farm operations conforming to GAAMPs
   - May apply to urban agriculture / native plantings
   - Check Lansing urban ag ordinances

2. **Michigan Natural Resources and Environmental Protection Act (NREPA)**
   — MCL 324.101 et seq.
   - Native species protections
   - Pollinator habitat protections

3. **First Amendment / Free Expression**
   - Landscaping as protected expression (limited case law, but growing)
   - City of Lansing v. homeowner precedent?

4. **Equal Protection / Selective Enforcement**
   - Are non-native invasive species (buckthorn, honeysuckle, etc.)
     tolerated while native plants are cited?
   - Are ornamental grasses tolerated at the same height?
   - Discriminatory enforcement based on aesthetic preference vs. ecology

5. **Easement Scope Limitation**
   - Easement holder's rights are limited to the purpose of the easement
   - Utility easement = right to access/maintain utility infrastructure
   - Does NOT grant right to dictate vegetation type/height unless
     vegetation interferes with utility function
   - Burden on enforcer to prove interference

6. **Michigan Noxious Weed Act** — MCL 247.61-71
   - Defines specific noxious weeds (typically invasive species)
   - Native plants are NOT noxious weeds
   - If ordinance uses "weed" language, challenge the classification

7. **Void for Vagueness**
   - If the ordinance says "too tall" or "unsightly" without specific
     measurable standards, it may be unconstitutionally vague

8. **Preemption**
   - State pollinator protection policies may preempt local aesthetic
     ordinances
   - Michigan Pollinator Protection Plan (2019)

### Federal defenses (if applicable):

9. **Endangered Species Act** — if any plantings support threatened pollinators
10. **Clean Water Act** — if plantings serve stormwater management function
