## Mission

Inkfish integrates vessels, engineering, medical innovation, and
operational expertise to develop new technologies and further marine
research initiatives. All data collected contributes to open-sourced
repositories. The organization works closely with host countries.

**Key quote** (Stu Buckle): "This has been a unique opportunity to
involve the researchers to collaborate with the ship builder and the
operators from the first stage of the design process."

## How Scientists Get Aboard

Based on expedition reports and team composition, Inkfish uses several models:

1. **Academic collaboration**: PIs bring their lab teams (e.g., Bongaerts
   brings Reefscape Genomics Lab from Cal Academy). Inkfish provides vessel
   time, logistics, submersibles. Scientists come from partner institutions.

2. **Expedition contractors**: Specialists hired for specific roles —
   dive safety officers, media, logistics. Some become long-term (Edd
   Stockdale, Jenn Thomson).

3. **Inkfish staff scientists**: Very few (3 in "Research" department).
   The org is structured as a services company that enables other
   scientists' research.

4. **Sponsored researchers**: Appears to be an emerging model. With
   RV6000 (70 berths) and RV11000 (130 berths), they'll have capacity
   for more independent scientists.

**Implication for Kevin**: The most natural entry is as a collaborating
scientist or expedition contractor — bringing both wet lab skills and
sovereign compute as a package. Not a traditional job application but
a "here's what I bring, let me aboard" pitch.

## Computational Infrastructure (Speculative)

Inkfish doesn't publicly describe their data infrastructure, but we
can infer from expedition reports:

- **On-vessel**: Labs equipped for sample processing, photography,
  preliminary sorting. Likely running standard laptops/workstations.
  RV11000 spec mentions "dedicated data-processing facilities."
- **Sequencing**: Almost certainly done shore-side at partner institutions
  (Cal Academy, NTNU, etc.) — too expensive/delicate for shipboard.
- **Analysis**: Shore-side HPC at partner institutions. Multi-week
  delay from sample collection to analysis results.
- **Storage**: Data on expedition NAS/drives, transferred on return.
- **Connectivity**: Satellite bandwidth only. Multi-month expeditions
  in remote Pacific locations = minimal cloud access.

**Where ecoPrimals fits**: The gap between sample collection and
analysis results. On-vessel GPU compute could provide:
- Same-day eDNA metabarcoding results (target species detection)
- Preliminary genomic analysis while still in the field
- Cryptographic provenance from sample to sequence to analysis
- Zero-connectivity operation with sync-on-return
