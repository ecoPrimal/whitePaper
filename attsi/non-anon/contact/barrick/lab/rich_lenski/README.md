## The Legend

Rich Lenski started the LTEE on February 24, 1988, with 12 flasks of *E. coli* B strain REL606. Thirty-seven years and 82,000+ generations later, it is the longest-running and most-cited experiment in evolutionary biology.

### Career Arc
- **1988**: Started LTEE at UC Irvine
- **1991**: Moved to MSU — the LTEE moved with him
- **1998**: Elected to National Academy of Sciences
- **2012**: Published "Genomic analysis of a key innovation in an experimental E. coli population" (*Nature*) — the Cit+ citrate-using lineage at 31,000 generations
- **2015**: Stepped back from daily bench work (Jeff Barrick took over operations)
- **2017**: MacArthur Fellowship (the "Genius Grant")
- **2022**: Officially retired from bench; Barrick moved LTEE to UT Austin
- **2025**: LTEE returns to MSU with Barrick

### Major LTEE Findings
1. **Parallel fitness increase**: All 12 populations increased fitness ~70% by 50,000 generations, with diminishing-returns dynamics
2. **Citrate utilization (Cit+)**: One population (Ara-3) evolved the ability to grow on citrate — a "key innovation" requiring multiple mutations in a specific historical order
3. **Convergent molecular evolution**: Independent populations hit the same genes (e.g., *spoT*, *topA*, *pykF*) at different sites
4. **Diminishing returns epistasis**: Early mutations have large fitness effects; later mutations have smaller effects in the context of earlier ones
5. **Historical contingency**: Cit+ required specific precursor mutations — "replay the tape of evolution" and it might not happen again
6. **Hypermutator lineages**: Several populations evolved elevated mutation rates (defective mismatch repair) — accelerates adaptation but increases hitchhiking load

### His Intellectual Framework

Lenski thinks about evolution through:
- **Fitness landscapes** (from Sewall Wright): populations climbing peaks, getting stuck, occasionally finding new peaks
- **Replicate populations**: The LTEE's power is the 12 replicates — convergence reveals constraint, divergence reveals contingency
- **Simplicity as virtue**: The LTEE is intentionally minimal (glucose-limited DM25 medium, 37°C, 1:100 daily dilution) — complexity is in the biology, not the environment
- **Time**: Just keep going. The LTEE's greatest insight is patience

## What to Talk About (30 Minutes)

This is 30 minutes with a legend. He is emeritus — not running experiments, not managing grants. He is thinking about the big picture. Your conversation should be about **ideas**.

### 1. Constrained Evolution
Your thesis is basically a formalization of what Lenski has been observing for 37 years: evolution is not random — it's constrained by the starting genotype, the environment, and the interactions between existing mutations. The LTEE data is the empirical proof. You can discuss this as a shared observation from different angles — he from decades of experimental data, you from computational formalization.

### 2. Anderson Localization as a Null Hypothesis
The Anderson framework gives a physics-grounded prediction for *when* constrained evolution should produce convergent solutions vs. divergent ones. This is new — Lenski's framework is phenomenological (observe convergence, infer constraint). Anderson localization provides a mechanistic prediction: low disorder → extended states → convergent outcomes; high disorder → localized states → divergent outcomes.

### 3. The Frozen Fossil Record
You have specific computational hypotheses about what the frozen fossil record should show (from `02_ltee_extensions.md`). Lenski will appreciate that someone has specific, testable predictions about his data rather than vague admiration.

### 4. What Comes Next
The LTEE is at 82,000+ generations. What questions remain? What new measurements should be made? Lenski has been thinking about this for decades — ask him.

### 5. Science Communication
Lenski is a prolific science communicator (blog since 2013, popular press, public debates). Your primals.eco / lab.primals.eco approach to publishing work-in-progress computational notebooks is aligned with his philosophy.
