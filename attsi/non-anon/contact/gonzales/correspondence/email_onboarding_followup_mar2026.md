Dr. Gonzales and Dr. Lisabeth,

Thank you both for your time and the conversation. I've been thinking
about it since — both the position and the science are genuinely
exciting to me.

Dr. Lisabeth, our discussion about binding specificity and low-affinity
interactions sent me down a productive path. I put together some
computational tools that extend what we talked about — modeling how
weak, distributed binders behave differently from strong specific ones,
and what that means for screening and drug repurposing. It connects
directly to the ADDRC compound library and the kind of dose-response
data your facility generates.

Dr. Gonzales, our conversation about where the lab is heading — the
cross-species iPSC models, the neuro-immune work, building the Drug
Discovery Program with the incoming faculty — is the kind of
opportunity I've been looking for. A new program organizing itself has
the freedom to do things the right way from the start: connecting
computation to bench work, building collaborations across departments,
and generating the preliminary data that funds the real experiments.

I've attached a folder with some materials I put together. These extend
the computational brief I shared earlier, building on the conversations
we had:

- **Lab Onboarding Package** — what I bring on Day 1 (bench and
  computational), how it connects to the lab's priorities, and how we
  can use it to engage other MSU teams.
- **Pre-Grant Pipeline** — a proposal for running pilot experiments on
  public data before any lab resources are spent. The computation is
  essentially free; the results become preliminary data for grants.
- **Cross-Species Causal Model** — the framework for asking why cat
  and dog skin respond differently to the same drug, and how tissue
  geometry might be the answer.
- **Data Sources & Integration** — a catalog of open data we can start
  analyzing immediately (GEO, PDB, ChEMBL, GTEx, and others).
- **Pilot Experiments** — 14 specific computational experiments with
  timelines, data sources, and the grant proposals they support.
- **Cost & Compute Brief** — the hardware and infrastructure details,
  including how we'd use ICER.

These are the systems I was alluding to when we talked about my
personal project. Everything is open-source and public — you can share
it with anyone on the team or in the department.

Every computational result is validated against published experimental
data. The pipelines reproduce known values from peer-reviewed papers and
fail explicitly when they don't match. Nothing is taken on faith;
everything is testable.

This work is AI-accelerated — it's how I develop and how I'd like to work
in the lab. I'm happy to help the team adopt data-secure AI workflows
as well.

I look forward to the opportunity to work with you both. The chance to
join a program at this stage — where we can shape how computation and
bench work fit together, build connections with the new faculty coming
in, and tackle real problems in drug discovery — is exactly what I've
been working toward.

Thank you again for your time.

Kevin Mok
mokkevin@msu.edu
