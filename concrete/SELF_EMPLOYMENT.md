## The Bottom Line

You don't need to "apply" for self-employment in Michigan. You just... start.

What makes it real:

2. The IRS recognizes you as self-employed when net earnings ≥ $400
3. Michigan has no additional sole proprietor registration requirement

## Tax Impact

### Self-Employment Tax
- 15.3% on net earnings (12.4% Social Security + 2.9% Medicare)
- You can deduct HALF of this on your 1040
- This builds Social Security quarters (401k distributions do NOT)

### 20% QBI Deduction
- As sole proprietor, deduct 20% of Qualified Business Income
- Made permanent by 2025 One Big Beautiful Bill Act
- Example: $50K net income → $10K QBI deduction → taxed on $40K

### Michigan State
- Flat 4.05% income tax
- No separate business filing for sole prop
- Home office deductions flow through from federal

### Quarterly Estimated Taxes
- If you expect to owe ≥ $1,000, pay quarterly
- Dates: Apr 15, Jun 17, Sep 16, Jan 15
- Form 1040-ES (federal) + MI-1040ES (state)
- Since you're starting mid-year, first payment is Sep 16, 2026

## Decision: Sole Prop vs LLC

| | Sole Prop | Single-Member LLC |
|---|---|---|
| Cost to start | $0 (+ $10 DBA optional) | $50 Michigan filing |
| Annual cost | $0 | $25 annual report |
| Liability protection | None (personal assets exposed) | Limited (separates business from personal) |
| Tax treatment | Schedule C (identical) | Schedule C (identical unless S-corp election) |
| Complexity | Minimal | Slightly more paperwork |
| When to upgrade | — | When hardware value + liability risk justifies it |

**Recommendation**: Start as sole prop today. File LLC later when first significant
income arrives (grant, prize, or client payment > $5K). The LLC protects the hardware
from business liability, which matters more once money is flowing.
