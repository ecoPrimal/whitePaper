# Autonomy and the Elegant Cage

---

## 1. The Poison of Benevolent Dependency

The most dangerous cage is not the one with iron bars, but the one with golden ones. It is the cage that is comfortable, convenient, and built by a benevolent keeper who promises to take care of you. This is the model of the modern cloud: a service so seamless and useful that you willingly build your life inside its walls, forgetting that the keeper of the cage also owns the lock.

This "benevolent dependency" is a poison. It trades short-term convenience for long-term sovereignty.

The `ecoPrimals` ethos rejects this model in its entirety.

## 2. The Ethical Mandate: Build Exits, Not Walls

Our core ethical commitment is to the **autonomy of the user**. Every component we build, from the smallest Primal to the installer itself, must adhere to a single, inviolable principle: **it must be designed to be left.**

This is not just a feature; it is a moral obligation.

-   **Data Must Be Portable:** All data stored within a `biomeOS` instance must be exportable in open, standard formats. There will be no proprietary data silos. The user must always be able to pack their digital life and move to another system—even a competing one—without penalty.
-   **Functionality Must Be Forkable:** The AGPL 3.0 license is our legal backstop, but our architectural commitment goes deeper. We will never design a system whose core functionality depends on a centralized, secret service that only we control. Another user must always have the technical and legal right to fork the entire stack, spin up their own version, and become a sovereign peer.
-   **No Secret Handshakes:** There will be no "special access" for first-party Primals. The APIs that `nestgate` uses to talk to `songbird` must be the same public, documented APIs available to any third-party developer.

We are not building a kingdom, no matter how elegant. We are building a road out of the wilderness. And that road must be open for all to travel, in any direction they choose. We do not build walls. We build exits.
