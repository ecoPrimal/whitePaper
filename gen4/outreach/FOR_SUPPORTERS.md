# For Supporters

**How to Contribute to the Ecosystem and What Your Contribution Enables**

**Date**: May 7, 2026
**Audience**: Anyone who sees value in sovereign open science and wants
to help sustain it

---

## The Short Version

The ecoPrimals ecosystem runs on consumer hardware — 11 nodes, ~$15K
in equipment. It serves a bioinformatics research community (ABG) with
free compute, validated pipelines, and full provenance infrastructure.
The monthly metabolic cost to keep it alive is ~$485 (electricity,
internet, depreciation).

Contributions of money, hardware, or time keep the ecosystem running
and growing. Every contribution is permanently attributed via sweetGrass
braids and Loam Certificates.

---

## What Your Contribution Does

### Money

| Amount | What It Covers |
|--------|---------------|
| $10/month | Electricity for one node for ~2 weeks |
| $50/month | Full metabolic cost of the dedicated ABG research node |
| $200 one-time | 1 TB NVMe drive for fast research scratch storage |
| $500 one-time | 4 TB HDD for cold dataset storage |
| $1,000 one-time | A configured gate shipped to a researcher who needs it |

### Hardware

| Item | What It Becomes |
|------|----------------|
| Spare GPU (any generation) | Work card in the float pool — powers ABG validation runs |
| Old tower / NUC | New gate in the mesh — more science capacity |
| NVMe / HDD | Storage where research workloads need it |
| Networking gear | 10G backbone expansion |

Hardware is registered in NestGate and BLAKE3-hashed. Your name is in
the attribution braid of every computation that hardware performs.

### Time and Expertise

| Contribution | Effect |
|-------------|--------|
| Science (validate a paper, run an analysis) | Sediment layer in the foundation |
| Code (spring binary, primal feature, tool) | AGPL-licensed ecosystem expansion |
| Documentation (wateringHole, whitePaper) | Better guidance for everyone |
| Outreach (connect a researcher, a lab, a PI) | Network growth |
| Sysadmin (configure a gate, debug a deploy) | Infrastructure strength |

## What You Get Back

### Structural Attribution

Your contribution gets a Loam Certificate — a permanent, hash-chained
record that tracks what you gave and what it enabled over time. This
is not a donor list on a website. It's a provenance record in the
geological layer of the ecosystem.

A $200 NVMe donation's certificate might eventually read:

```
Certificate: Contribution
  contributor: your_name
  type: hardware
  value: "1 TB NVMe (Samsung 990 EVO)"
  date: 2026-05-07
  enabled:
    ├── 47 ABG validation runs
    ├── 3 publications under scyBorg
    ├── 12,000 BLAKE3-hashed datasets
    └── foundation sediment layers 12–58
```

The certificate ferments — it grows as the hardware it funded produces
more science.

### Visibility Into Impact

The shared workspace model means you can see what the infrastructure
produces. ABG validation runs, foundation sediment layers, publications
— all visible, all attributed.

### Protection Under scyBorg

Everything the ecosystem produces is triple copyleft (AGPL + ORC +
CC-BY-SA). No one — including the infrastructure operator — can take
the work private. Your contribution funds commons that stay common.

## What This Is Not

**Not a startup investment.** There is no equity, no cap table, no
exit. The ecosystem cannot be acquired because it's AGPL.

**Not a charity donation.** You get structural attribution that
compounds over time, not a tax receipt.

**Not a subscription.** There is no paywall, no premium tier, no
"supporter-only" access. The infrastructure is open to everyone
regardless of contribution.

**Not cryptocurrency.** No tokens, no coins, no speculative assets.
Money is money. Hardware is hardware.

## Where Contributions Go

The ecosystem's metabolic costs:

| Cost | Monthly |
|------|---------|
| Electricity (11 nodes) | ~$150 |
| Internet | ~$80 |
| Domain / DNS | ~$5 |
| Hardware depreciation | ~$250 |
| **Total** | **~$485** |

100% of monetary contributions go to metabolic costs and hardware
growth until the ecosystem's products (esotericWebb, helixVision,
blueFish) generate their own revenue through the sunCloud economic
model. At that point, the flywheel transitions to self-sustaining
economics and supporter contributions fund expansion rather than
survival.

See `gen4/economics/THE_FLYWHEEL.md` for the full economic model and

## The Bidirectional Vision

Today, contributions flow inward — supporters help the ecosystem
survive and grow. Tomorrow, the ecosystem generates surplus and
sends configured gates outward to researchers who need them.

A $500–1,000 configured node (recycled tower, NUC, whatever fits)
with NUCLEUS pre-deployed, shipped to a researcher at a university
in a resource-constrained setting, gives them $50K+ in equivalent
compute value. Their science flows back as foundation sediment.
sweetGrass connects the hardware donor → the operator → the
researcher → the science.

Contributions today become infrastructure for researchers who don't
have it yet.
