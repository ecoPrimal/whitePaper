## From Bibliography to Bedrock

Science has a citation problem. A paper cites another paper. That paper
cites a dataset. The dataset was generated by software that was compiled
on a system that no longer exists, from source code that was never
versioned, using parameters copied from a PDF that was typeset from a
LaTeX document that referenced a spreadsheet maintained by a graduate
student who graduated in 2014.

This is what we called "jelly strings" — the invisible, fragile threads
that hold published knowledge together. They look solid in a bibliography.
They dissolve under structural pressure.

The ecoPrimals answer is not "better citations." It is **geology**.

---

## What Geology Means Here

In physical geology, the historical record is not written in books. It is
written in layers of rock. Each layer was deposited by a specific process
at a specific time. The layers compact under pressure, becoming
load-bearing structure for everything above. The record is not maintained
— it simply *is*. You can drill a core sample anywhere and read the
history backward, with every layer in chronological order, every
transition boundary marking a change in conditions.

This is what `sporeGarden/foundation` does for scientific computation.

### The Layers

**Bedrock**: NCBI genomes, UniProt proteomes, KEGG pathways, PDB
structures — the public, immutable reference data that exists independent
of any project. These are the continental plates. They move slowly. When
they do move (NCBI updates an assembly, UniProt revises a proteome), the
foundation records both the old and new state, because geology preserves
unconformities.

**Sedimentary Layers**: Each time a spring validates a published result
through NUCLEUS, it deposits a layer. The layer contains:
- What data was used (BLAKE3 hashes of every input)
- What computation ran (toadStool dispatch record)
- What result was produced (compared against the published target)
- Who was involved (sweetGrass attribution braid)
- When it happened (rhizoCrypt DAG timestamp → loamSpine permanent entry)

A single layer is thin — one run, one result. But layers accumulate.

**Metamorphic Layers**: When multiple springs validate the same target
independently — wetSpring running an ODE model on CPU, hotSpring running
barraCuda on GPU, both arriving at the same published cell cycle duration
— the overlapping sediment undergoes metamorphism. The confidence
increases. The layer compacts. What was once "we reproduced this" becomes
"this is a structural fact of the system."

**Igneous Intrusions**: Novel hypotheses that emerge from unexpected
cross-thread patterns. When Anderson localization (Thread 7) explains
both cytokine propagation in inflamed skin (Thread 3) and quorum sensing
in microbial mats (Thread 4), the intrusion cuts across existing layers,
creating new structure that cannot be explained by any single domain.

**Fossils**: The provenance records themselves — rhizoCrypt DAGs,
loamSpine entries, sweetGrass braids. They are preserved within the
layers, providing structural evidence of the process that deposited them.
Unlike physical fossils, computational fossils are exact: the DAG
Merkle root is a mathematical proof, not an impression.

## Why Not a Database?

Databases are maintained. They have schemas that change, migrations that
run, administrators who decide what to keep and what to archive. They are
institutions, not landscapes.

Foundation is a landscape. Nobody maintains a canyon — water cuts it.
Nobody maintains a sediment layer — pressure compacts it. The processes
are natural consequences of the system's operation.

When a spring developer runs `foundation_validate.sh`, they are not
"updating a database." They are depositing sediment. The layer forms
whether or not anyone is watching. The provenance trio (rhizoCrypt,
loamSpine, sweetGrass) records the event the way heat and pressure record
metamorphism — structurally, permanently, without narrative.

## The Sediment Cycle

The cycle is:

1. **Foundation declares** — data source manifests and validation targets
2. **NUCLEUS deploys** — primals on gates via projectNUCLEUS
3. **Springs execute** — computations through toadStool with provenance
4. **Sediment deposits** — results recorded with BLAKE3, DAG, ledger, braid
5. **Foundation absorbs** — targets marked validated, thread confidence grows
6. **Springs evolve** — absorb validation patterns, rewire to full NUCLEUS composition
7. **The deposit strengthens** — cross-spring metamorphism, layer compaction
8. **Products consume** — validated geology as provable substrate

This cycle runs continuously. It does not require coordination meetings,
status updates, or manual merges. It is geological — driven by the
natural operation of springs through NUCLEUS.

Springs strengthen foundation. Foundation strengthens springs. The
sediment accumulates. Living geology.

## From gen3 to gen4: The Transition

gen3 proved the instruments are in tune. 28 baseCamp papers validated
12,510+ checks across 8 springs. That work established the stratigraphic
column — we know which layers exist.

gen4 begins the active deposition cycle. The foundation repository
(`sporeGarden/foundation`) operationalizes the column: machine-readable
manifests, automated data fetching, provenance-wrapped validation
pipelines, and workloads executable through toadStool.

The gen3-to-gen4 transition is the transition from **describing** the
geology to **building** it. The papers described what the layers should
contain. The foundation actually deposits them.

## What This Means for the Ecosystem

### For Springs

Your validation runs are not throwaway CI. They are geological events.
Every [OK] deposits a layer. Every cross-spring confirmation
metamorphoses a layer. Build your validation binaries knowing that their
output becomes part of the permanent record.

### For Primals

NestGate stores the artifacts. rhizoCrypt records the DAG. loamSpine
commits the Merkle root. sweetGrass braids the attribution. Your
primitives are the geological processes — the heat, pressure, water, and
wind that form the layers. The geology could not exist without you.

### For Products

You build on geology, not promises. When helixVision displays a
protein structure, the structure's provenance chain reaches back through
the validation layers to the original PDB deposition, through the
UniProt annotation, through the spring validation, to the BLAKE3 hash
of the genome it was predicted from. Your users don't need to trust you.
They can audit the geology.

### For Collaborators

When you join a thread, you join a geological site. The core samples are
available. The deposition processes are documented. Your work adds new
layers. The attribution braid ensures your contribution is structurally
recorded — not as a name in an acknowledgments section, but as a
provenance event in the permanent ledger.
